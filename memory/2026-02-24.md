## Paper Discussion — UL & Latent Forcing
- Yu asked about "Unified Latents (UL): How to train your latents" (arXiv:2602.17270, Google DeepMind, 2026-02-19)
  - Core: jointly regularize encoder with diffusion prior, decoder is diffusion model, encoder noise = prior min noise → tight bitrate bound
  - Key result: ImageNet-512 FID 1.4, Kinetics-600 FVD 1.3 SOTA
  - Still two-stage training (end-to-end single-stage didn't match FID), but objective is unified
  - No semantic latent analysis (no DINO/linear probe tests) — it's a better compression scheme, not representation learning
  - No order-related contributions (standard reverse diffusion trajectory)
- Compared with "Latent Forcing" (arXiv:2602.11401): both address VAE+diffusion disconnect
  - Latent Forcing: back to pixel space, latent as scratchpad with separate noise schedules (engineering trick)
  - UL: stay in latent space, unify objective function (theory-driven)
  - UL is more principled; Latent Forcing's ordering insight connects to Yu's "Thinking Out of Order" work
- Why VAE+diffusion can't train end-to-end: gradient disconnected (latents precomputed), VRAM explosion, training instability (moving target), objective conflict (reconstruction vs simple distribution)

## Paper Review — Oracle Sampler (arXiv:2602.19619)
- Tang et al. — "Are Discrete Diffusion Samplers Correct?"
- Core: HMM oracle framework isolates sampler error from denoiser error
- Key finding: few-step dLLM sampler incorrect, converges only when steps ≈ T (degenerates to AR)
- GenPPL is gameable (temperature sharpening), MAUVE insensitive to transition-level bias
- LLaDA has interesting failure: oracle breaks confidence-based remasking coupling
- Yu's take: too many experiments, unclear "so what" for ReMDM variants and temperature ablations

## Paper Review — "Thinking Out of Order" (arXiv:2601.22035) — Yu's own paper
- Yu Fu, Greg Ver Steeg et al.
- Core: dLLM confidence-based remasking enables order-robust reasoning (answer-first doesn't hurt)
- AR drops 67% on answer-first, LLaDA only 4%
- Dream (AR-distilled) shows intermediate behavior → order robustness depends on training, not just architecture
- ReasonOrderQA benchmark with D1-D4 difficulty levels
- My review: core insight clean, but GSM8K/Math500 results redundant; D1 too easy, D4 too hard; breakdown conditions somewhat obvious; needs stronger motivation for real-world use cases; lacks theoretical bound
